## Module 1

### Summative 2 — Integrative team discussion report

A group report documenting an integrative discussion held by the team, structured as formal
meeting minutes. The assignment asks the team to introduce the leadership constructs covered in
the module, summarise the assigned readings, and then reflect on its own experience across three
guiding questions: **facts**, **comparisons**, and **opportunities for growth and change**.

**Required reading.** Chapter 8, *Leading Groups*, in Keyton, J., & Beck, S. J. (2023),
*Communicating in Groups and Teams: Strategic Interactions* (5th ed.), Cognella Academic
Publishing.

The report also draws on Carson, Tesluk & Marrone (2007) on shared leadership, Wang, Waldman &
Zhang (2014) on shared leadership and team effectiveness, and Edmondson (1999) on psychological
safety.

## Module 2

### Summative 4 — Integrative team discussion report

The same deliverable format as Module 1, applied to a second integrative discussion. Because this is
no longer the first module, the report must also trace the connections back to the constructs
studied previously — in this case, showing how the relational competence and psychological safety
of Module 1 underpin the communication climate and trust examined here.

**Required reading.** Chapter 6, *Building Relationships in Groups*, in Keyton, J., & Beck, S. J.
(2023), *Communicating in Groups and Teams: Strategic Interactions* (5th ed.), Cognella Academic
Publishing.

**Research articles.**

- Buvik, M. P., & Tvedt, S. D. (2017) — trust, commitment and knowledge sharing in project teams.
- Liao, G. Y., Pham, T. T. L., Cheng, T. C. E., & Teng, C. I. (2020) — participation, team
  identification and commitment, from a social identity perspective.
- Uddin, M. A., Mahmood, M., & Fan, L. (2019) — employee engagement, commitment, organisational
  citizenship behaviour and team performance.

## Module 3

### Summative 6 — Integrative team discussion report

The same deliverable format, applied to group decision making. The report connects the decision
procedures of this module to the shared leadership and psychological safety of Module 1 and to the
trust and knowledge sharing of Module 2. The course's TechFin Solutions case study on an agile
transformation served as the trigger for the discussion.

**Required reading.** Chapter 5, *Decision Making*, in Keyton, J., & Beck, S. J. (2023),
*Communicating in Groups and Teams: Strategic Interactions* (5th ed.), Cognella Academic
Publishing.

**Research articles.**

- Tabassi, A. A., Abdullah, A., & Bryde, D. J. (2019) — conflict management, team coordination and
  performance in multicultural temporary projects.
- Guinan, P. J., Parise, S., & Langowitz, N. (2019) — levers for building an innovative digital
  project team.
- Hidalgo, E. S. (2019) — adapting Scrum to manage a distributed research initiative.

The Module 3 to 5 folders hold no rubric images; the grading criteria below are assumed to match
those of the earlier modules.

## Module 6 — Final team project

The closing module has no new reading. It consolidates the course in two assessments, worth 10 % and 30 % of
the final grade respectively. Canvas shows no due date for either.

### Summative 11 — Self- and peer-assessment (30 points)

An individual Excel form provided by the course. Each student scores themselves and every teammate on six
criteria (attendance and punctuality, contribution to discussions, completing tasks on time, weekly preparation,
cooperative attitude, contribution to the project's success), each on a 0/1/3/4/5 scale, and may add a free-text
comment. Each member submits their own file.

### Summative 12 — Portfolio (138 points)

A group report built on the three research articles of one module. The team chose **Module 3** (team dynamics and
decision making). For each article the report identifies one construct with three subcomponents, the article's
recommendations, and at least three possible applications in organisations the team knows. It also includes an
introduction that ties the readings together, the three main things the team learned during the course (including
which assumptions about team leadership changed), a conclusion summarising the readings, and an index.

**Articles.**

- Tabassi, A. A., Abdullah, A., & Bryde, D. J. (2019) — leader's conflict management style, team coordination and
  performance in multicultural temporary construction projects.
- Guinan, P. J., Parise, S., & Langowitz, N. (2019) — levers for building an innovative digital project team.
- Hidalgo, E. S. (2019) — adapting Scrum to a distributed research initiative.

**Rubric — 138 points.**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Per article: subcomponents 1–3 (three or more relevant points each), recommendations (three or more), applications (three or more) — 5 × 6 points × 3 articles | 90 |
| Main learnings (at least three) | 24 |
| Introduction, index and conclusion | 12 |
| Writing and spelling | 6 |
| Formal requirements (format, APA, deadline) | 6 |

**Formal requirements.** PDF, at most 12 letter-size pages including the bibliography, Arial or Calibri 11 pt,
1.15 line spacing, justified text, filename `PMG531_MX_Grupo_XX`. Each construct should run to between 500 and
1,000 words, and the introduction, learnings and conclusion to about 1,000 words each.

## Learning outcome and rubric

The Module 1 to 5 reports evaluate the same learning outcome and share an identical rubric.

**Learning outcome assessed (AE1).** Analyse the challenges involved in managing a project team,
considering its development and the strategies needed to respond effectively to organisational
needs.

**Grading rubric — 72 points total**

| Criterion | Points | Requirement for full marks |
|---|---:|---|
| Facts | 12 | Three or more relevant facts or personal experiences |
| Comparison | 36 | Three experiences from the literature compared against the team's own, with three similarities or differences each |
| Opportunities | 12 | Three or more opportunities for improvement drawn from what was learned |
| Writing and spelling | 6 | No spelling or drafting errors |
| Formal requirements | 6 | Format, APA style, and submission deadline |

**Formal requirements.** Submitted as PDF; at most 7 letter-size pages including the
bibliography; Arial or Calibri 11 pt; 1.15 line spacing; justified text. The filename must follow
the pattern `CourseCode_MX_Res_Grupo_XX`, with no accents, no `ñ`, a single separator character,
and no more than 30 characters. Each section of the report should run to between 200 and 500
words.
